## Context

What forces are in play? What did we know, and not know, at the time? State the
problem so that a reader who disagrees with the decision can still see why it
was a real question. If a specification constrains the answer, say which clause
and what it actually requires — quote it rather than paraphrasing.

## Decision

What we are doing, in the present tense and in one or two sentences, followed by
the detail. Include the options rejected and the reason each lost; an ADR whose
alternatives section is empty is usually a decision nobody actually made.

## Consequences

What becomes easier, what becomes harder, and what we now have to maintain.
Include the operational and testing consequences, not only the design ones. Name
the beads that inherit work from this decision.
